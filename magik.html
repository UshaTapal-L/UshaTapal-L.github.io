The short version

Campaign managers didn't need more charts. They needed to know which customers to target, with what offer, and why. I designed a “Decision-First” framework that turns streams of telemetry into plain-language recommendations, wrapped in guardrails so non-technical users can trust and control the AI that acts on them.

01 Context

Telecom operators run thousands of marketing campaigns a year: data bundles for heavy users, win-back offers for customers about to leave, loyalty rewards for high-value subscribers. This is Customer Value Management (CVM), and it lives on top of huge volumes of network and usage data.

Magik was 6D's platform for this work. It was built as a telemetry tool: powerful, but it showed users data and expected them to work out the decision themselves. The business goal was to move it to an autonomous, AI-driven marketing application, serving operators across the Middle East, Africa, Asia and Europe.

My brief

Redesign Magik so that AI does the heavy analysis, while marketers stay confidently in control of what goes out to millions of subscribers.

02 The problem

Data first, decision last

Users had to read dense telemetry, filter it, build a segment and only then decide on a campaign. The insight was there, buried several screens deep.

Multi-step logic, by hand

Building a campaign meant stitching together audience rules, offers, channels and timing. Every step was a chance to make a costly mistake at operator scale.

AI without trust

Moving to AI-driven campaigns raised a new question: how does a non-technical marketer understand, check and override what the AI proposes?

How might we help campaign managers act on the right opportunity in minutes, while the AI does the analysis and the human keeps the final say?

03 Research

Stakeholder interviews

With product, data science and client-facing teams, to understand goals and constraints.

Personas

Built around campaign managers: their daily jobs, pressures and where they lose time.

Competitor IA study

Mapped how CVM, martech and analytics products structure navigation and campaign flows.

AI-assisted desk research

Used AI tools to synthesize industry sources quickly, then validated themes with users.

What we learned

  1. 01
    Users think in goals, not metrics. “Reduce churn in prepaid users” is the job; the telemetry is just evidence.
  2. 02
    Most time goes on interpretation. The slow part isn't launching a campaign, it's figuring out which one to launch.
  3. 03
    Trust needs a “why”. Users accept AI suggestions when they can see the reasoning and change the inputs.
  4. 04
    Competitors still lead with dashboards. Very few products made the recommendation itself the main interface, which was our opening.

04 The Decision-First framework

I flipped the order of the experience. Instead of starting with data and ending with a decision, every screen starts with the decision and keeps the data one click away as evidence.

Principle 1

Lead with the action

Each recommendation reads like a sentence: who, what offer, which channel, expected outcome.

Principle 2

Evidence on demand

Progressive disclosure keeps charts and telemetry behind the recommendation, not in front of it.

Principle 3

Guardrails, not black boxes

Visible limits, previews and approvals make the AI's actions predictable and reversible.

06 Design system

An AI product that changes quickly needs a stable base. I built and run Magik's centralized, tokenized design system in Figma, covering components, typography and colour, so designers and engineers share one source of truth.

Building it as tokens meant decisions like colour or spacing could change in one place and flow through every product in the CVM suite, which cut implementation back-and-forth between design and engineering.

07 Impact

30%

Reduction in task completion time after moving core workflows to Decision-First.

30%

Faster for non-technical users to build, audit and deploy multi-channel campaigns in the agent workspace.

Beyond the numbers, the design system and the framework gave the wider CVM suite a shared way to present AI, so new AI features now ship with consistent patterns instead of one-off screens.

08 What I learned

  • Design the decision before the data. Once we agreed on the decision a screen supports, deciding what data to show became easy.
  • Trust is a UX problem. Explanations, previews and undo did more for AI adoption than accuracy alone.
  • Systems make speed possible. The token-based design system let a small team keep up with a fast-moving AI roadmap.
